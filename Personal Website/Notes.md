## Sections

- **1. Hero / Intro Section**
    
    - **Purpose:** The immediate elevator pitch. Within three seconds, visitors should know who you are and what you build.
        
    - **Content:** Your name, a compelling professional title (e.g., _Principal Software Engineer & Architect_), a short one-sentence tagline about your core focus (e.g., _Building resilient cloud-native infrastructure and secure distributed systems_), and primary call-to-action (CTA) buttons like **"Get in Touch"** and **"View Resume"**.
        
- **2. About / Core Expertise**
    
    - **Purpose:** To highlight your technical identity and years of experience without forcing people to read a dense paragraph.
        
    - **Content:** A brief, punchy bio paired with a clean **tech stack grid or badges** (e.g., Go, Kubernetes, OAuth/OIDC, Distributed Systems, Cloud Architecture) so scanners can instantly gauge your technical alignment.
        
- **3. Featured Projects / Case Studies**
    
    - **Purpose:** Proof of work. This is the most critical section for engineering roles.
        
    - **Content:** 3 to 4 standout projects. Instead of just listing code repositories, frame them as mini case studies:
        
        - **The Problem:** What scale, architecture, or security challenge did you solve?
            
        - **Your Solution:** The technical approach, patterns used (e.g., concurrent pipeline design, zero-trust patterns), and your specific role.
            
        - **The Impact:** Metrics, performance gains, or architectural resilience achieved. Include live links, GitHub repos, or architecture diagrams where applicable.
            
- **4. Professional Experience / Career Highlights**
    
    - **Purpose:** A high-level timeline of your career progression and leadership scope.
        
    - **Content:** Instead of dumping your entire resume here, highlight your most recent or impactful roles (e.g., companies, key achievements, system-level impact). Include a downloadable PDF resume link for recruiters who need the formal document.
        
- **5. Writing & Thought Leadership _(Optional but Powerful)_**
    
    - **Purpose:** Shows communication skills, deep domain knowledge, and passion for the craft.
        
    - **Content:** Links to technical blog posts, architectural deep-dives, whitepapers, or community contributions (such as standards working groups or open-source projects).
        
- **6. Contact & Footer**
    
    - **Purpose:** Low-friction conversion. Make it as easy as possible for a hiring manager to reach out.
        
    - **Content:** Direct email link, professional social profiles (LinkedIn, GitHub), and perhaps a modern copy-to-clipboard button for your email address.

## Implementation Guidelines

- **Keep it Lightweight:** Host it on a fast, modern platform (like Vercel, Netlify, or GitHub Pages) using a clean framework or static site generator. Performance is your first unspoken technical interview.
    
- **Design with Intent:** Keep the UI minimalist, accessible (high contrast, clean typography), and fully responsive for mobile.
